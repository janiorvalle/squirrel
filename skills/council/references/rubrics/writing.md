# Rubric: writing

For a doc, a readme, product copy, an email, an error message, a skill file. Seven categories, each scored 1 to 10, then Overall, your judgment of the whole, not an average.

**Purpose.** The piece is one kind of document, tutorial, how-to, reference, or explanation, and stays that kind. It knows who reads it and what they will do next. The first sentence is the answer or the outcome, not a run-up.

**Structure.** Headings say what the section contains. Steps come in the order the reader performs them. Parallel items are a list, a line of argument is prose. Nothing is said twice.

**Sentences.** One thing per sentence, about twenty words, with a verb. Short and long alternate. No sentence joins two ideas with a dash or a semicolon that should have been a period.

**Word choice.** The everyday word unless the long one buys precision. No machine vocabulary, no puffery, no promotional words. The same thing goes by the same name throughout.

**Precision.** No sentence open to two readings. Every instruction names the thing on the reader's screen. Numbers and commands are exact and stand on their own line or in a code block. Nothing is hedged that the writer knows.

**Voice.** Reads as one direct, warm, technically grounded person. No bold on every noun, no forced threes, no not-X-but-Y, no one-line closers, no chatbot residue. Sentence case headings. An opinion where the kind allows one.

**Completeness.** The reader can take their next step from the text alone. Prerequisites are stated. The failure cases the reader will hit are covered. Nothing links out for something the piece should have said.

**Overall.** Would you hand this to a new reader as an example of the kind done right? 10 means yes without caveats.
